                                                Abstract
           We construct a finite, source-hereditary descent for shaded Kakeya incidence measures.
      A symmetric joint packet law keeps the forgotten translation fibre, all support marginals,
      and every later restriction in one probability space. Exact-support determinants, literal face
      deletion, root exchange, conditional insertion kernels, and a reduced history tree then reduce
      the global filtered estimate to one root-cross physical energy.
           The analytic closure is geometric. The planar Córdoba estimate [2] gives the two-
      dimensional base. In dimension three the sticky Kakeya theorem and its sticky/non-sticky
      reduction close the root gate. At the next handoff, directionwise sampling and an unbiased
      fibre lift reduce the remaining power-small source to the marked four-dimensional sticky
      estimate of [9]. Hence the scalar descent closes through dimension four; in dimensions d ≥ 5
      the analogous root-cross gate remains an input.
           The Selmer–Cartan tower [1] is used only as structural provenance for this concrete
      filtration: its all-support comparison identifies the determinant successor and records coeffi-
      cient, confluence, and root–Moore labels without turning them into physical multiplicity. It
      supplies no Kakeya estimate. The hereditary typed statement has the further, independent
      requirement of a positive Hall/Radon–Nikodym capacity on correlated pair sources; this is
      not implied by the tower or by the scalar sticky input.

Standing inputs and scope. The physical source is a finite direction-separated family of
shaded δ-tubes. All branches and deletions are measured in the single incidence law (21) and its
symmetric packet extension (17). The initial hereditary shading density obeys λ−1      in = δ
                                                                                               −o(1) for

the subpower formulation; otherwise its reciprocal is retained explicitly. The analytic inputs are
the hereditary planar Córdoba estimate [2], the three-dimensional sticky and sticky/non-sticky
theorems [7, 10], and the uniform marked four-dimensional estimate [9]. These close (142) in
dimensions two, three, and four, respectively.
     For compatibility with [1], fix finite support, coefficient, confluence, and Cartan ceilings and a
finite set of odd coefficient primes. The tower supplies the normalized all-support controller and
its Moore–Reedy reconstruction. In particular, a q 2 line records supplied confluence provenance;
it is not the physical pair multiplicity and is never used as an analytic estimate. The scalar
proof below needs only the concrete support filtration, the exact-support/face decomposition,
the hereditary source ledger, and proper-history recombination. The additional motivic labels
are carried passively and then removed by the normalized physical augmentation.

13     Closure of the filtered route
13.1    Structural bookkeeping
The fixed-root determinant comparison (8), literal side faces, root projection, insertion kernels,
and the symmetric packet law (17) verify the finite physical filtration. Global gluing and every
hereditary restriction are controlled by (24) and (25). The operators (27) give the structural
representation (28); the passive decoration base change (1) does not alter physical multiplicity.
   After the augmentation (31), the only analytic data are the exact-support determinant, the
confluence/load fibre, and the root antecedent. The reduced-history tree and (137) control
every strict predecessor. Thus the global confluence estimate R6 is equivalent to the remaining
physical root-cross gate (142); no formal motivic reconstruction is used to prove it.

13.2    Scalar analytic closure
In dimension two the hereditary planar Córdoba estimate [2] supplies the base case. In dimension
three, (52)–(59), together with the sticky inputs (177), close the root gate.
    At the three-to-four-dimensional handoff, the factorized and fibre-disjoint blocks close by
(108) and (109). Directionwise sampling and the unbiased lift close every shell covered by (91).
Proper-history fibre collisions are absorbed by (73); the residual low-occupancy source is exactly
(93). Its broad part is multilinear, its narrow part retains the affine fibre mark, and the uniform
marked theorem [9] supplies (81). Hence the scalar route closes through dimension four.
    For d ≥ 5, the analogous source-hereditary marked root gate remains an input. Assuming
(142) at every retained support and load stage, the support induction (125) gives

The final weighted-to-unweighted cutoff (130) then retains subpower incidence mass. For sparse
initial shading the same argument keeps the explicit density factors in (124); the filtration creates
no density.

13.3    The separate typed requirement
The hereditary typed statement requires a positive common-point routing on the correlated pair
source. Its exact forms are the Hall/Radon–Nikodym condition (160) and the equivalent capacity
system (207); the fractional-mode bound (204) is sufficient. The structural tower supplies labelled
destinations and boundaries, but neither this positivity nor the weighted capacity. Consequently
the scalar theorem above does not claim the typed comparison.
